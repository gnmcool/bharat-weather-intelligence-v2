## 1. Why D2 exists (prospective purpose)

D2 provides an **immutable, secondary rain reference for prospective M4.4-D verification** of CORE's issued items.
It has three uses and no others:

1. **Rain reference:** 24-h rain totals per **IST calendar day** at the 36 archive points.
2. **Horizon coverage:** every day that can fall in a prospective CORE issue's verification horizon **I+1 … I+6**
   (issues from 28 Sep 2026 onwards).
3. **Flood rain component:** the daily totals from which a later stage (D3) forms 72-h rain sums for the flood
   rain component, where applicable. D2 retrieves nothing extra for this.

**D2 must not:**
- modify CORE;
- modify V2 or `main`;
- alter the historical M4.2 releases or the M4.4-A/B/C results;
- introduce new verification metrics or change the M4.4-D methodology;
- fetch IMD data;
- retrieve any variable other than `total_precipitation` (no land-sea mask, no other ERA5 field);
- become general weather-model verification or general ERA5 analysis;
- fetch ERA5 during planning.

## 2. ERA5's role and relation to IMD

- **ERA5 is the secondary / interim rain reference** for prospective M4.4-D verification. It is labelled
  "ERA5 reanalysis (secondary)" in every file, manifest and downstream result.
- **IMD remains the eventual primary rain reference.** It is used once IMD publishes its 2026 gridded rainfall, as
  a separate versioned supplement matched as CORE day D ↔ IMD file D+1 (M4.4-D-SPEC-1.0, decision Q2).
  **D2 does not replace IMD and does not fetch it.**
- **ERA5 and IMD are never combined.** There are separate releases, separate case tables, and separate counts,
  metrics and maturity states, and no merged or "best available" reference.
- **Different day definitions, never merged:**
  - ERA5 in D2 uses the **IST calendar day** (§5.4), the same day definition as CORE's issued rain.
  - IMD uses the **08:30–08:30 IST rain day** (file D = 24 h ending 08:30 IST on D).
  - D2 produces no 08:30-window ERA5 product.
- **Relation to the historical ERA5 values.** The M4.4-B ERA5 values (secondary, historical) were obtained through
  Open-Meteo. D2 values come directly from the Copernicus Climate Data Store (CDS). The grid nodes may differ (§5.2).
  D2 does not try to match the Open-Meteo grid. The difference is recorded as provenance. Historical and prospective
  results are never combined.

## 4. Exact request

### 4.1 Request structure: one retrieval = two CDS requests

IST day d₁ of month M needs the stamps 19:00Z–23:00Z of the **last UTC day of month M−1** (§5.4). CDS requests take
`year`/`month`/`day`/`time` lists and return **every combination** of them, so a single request spanning two months
would return both months in full. In January it would also combine two years (Dec and Jan of both years). One
**retrieval** of month M is therefore exactly **two CDS requests**, each asking only for stamps that exist and are
needed:

| Request | `year` | `month` | `day` | `time` | Items (fields) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **boundary** | year of M−1 | M−1 | last day of M−1 | `19:00`, `20:00`, `21:00`, `22:00`, `23:00` | **5** |
| **month** | year of M | M | `01` … last day of M | `00:00` … `23:00` | **n × 24** (672–744) |

Both requests use the same fixed fields:

```python
dataset = "reanalysis-era5-single-levels"
request = {
    "product_type": ["reanalysis"],
    "variable": ["total_precipitation"],
    "year": [...], "month": [...], "day": [...], "time": [...],   # per the table above
    "data_format": "grib",
    "download_format": "unarchived",
    "area": [34.0, 72.25, 8.25, 94.75],                            # §4.2
}
```

- **Lists:** they are zero-padded strings in ascending order, so each request dict has one canonical form. Its
  SHA-256 (canonical JSON: sorted keys, no whitespace) is recorded before submission.
- **Coverage:** the month request's stamps 19:00Z–23:00Z of the last day of M are not used by month M. They are
  requested anyway, so the request stays a plain whole-month request. They are subject to the same `expver` check
  (§6).
- **Why not smaller:** a separate small box per point would need 36 area requests per request type (72 per
  retrieval). That contradicts the two-request retrieval and the job limits in §7, and it multiplies queued jobs. The
  single bounding box (§4.2) is the smallest structure that keeps one retrieval to two jobs.

### 4.2 Area

- **Rule (fixed):**
  - `North = ceil₀.₂₅(max point latitude)`;
  - `South = floor₀.₂₅(min point latitude)`;
  - `West = floor₀.₂₅(min point longitude)`;
  - `East = ceil₀.₂₅(max point longitude)`;
  - over the 36 points of `archive/points.json`, where `ceil₀.₂₅` / `floor₀.₂₅` round to the 0.25° grid.
- **Value** for `points.json` SHA-256 `4919f87259fd22a83a6050e01497f3be86711ce0e10e28aecbb9af324b65d49e`:
  `[34.0, 72.25, 8.25, 94.75]`, which is 104 × 91 = 9,464 nodes.
- **Refusal:** if `points.json` changes, the box is recomputed and the change is visible in the request hash. A
  changed points file is itself outside D2's scope and would need owner approval.
- The box contains every point's nearest node, including both candidate nodes of the tie in §5.2.

### 4.3 Recorded before and after submission
Recorded per request:
- the canonical request dict and its SHA-256;
- the CDS `request_id`;
- the item count (number of `time` × `day` values);
- the submission, start, completion and download timestamps;
- the queue and total duration;
- the downloaded bytes;
- the SHA-256 of the downloaded file.

These go in the job ledger (§7.3) and the manifest (§10.3). **The CDS token is never recorded anywhere.**

## 5. Extraction

### 5.1 Points
The **36 fixed points** of `archive/points.json`, in file order, the same file as the prospective archive. Its
SHA-256 is recorded in the manifest. The hourly and daily tables contain exactly these 36 point ids, in this order.

### 5.2 Grid node selection and the fixed tie-break

- **Nearest node, per axis (fixed rule):**
  - `node_lat = round_half_up(lat × 4) / 4`;
  - `node_lon = round_half_up(lon × 4) / 4`.
- **Arithmetic:** evaluated in exact decimal arithmetic on the coordinates as written in `points.json`, not in binary
  floating point.
- **Tie-break (permanent):** a coordinate exactly midway between two grid lines goes to the **northern** node
  (latitude) or the **eastern** node (longitude).
  - Among the current points this applies only to **IN-11-243 (South Sikkim), 27.32° N 88.375° E**. Its node is
    **27.25° N, 88.50° E**, at 14.601 km. The western candidate 88.25° E is also 14.601 km away.
  - The rule is part of this plan. Changing it requires a new plan revision and new versioned releases; existing
    releases are never re-derived in place.
- **Check against distance:** for all 36 current points, the per-axis rule selects a node at minimum haversine
  distance among the four surrounding nodes (computed; no exceptions).
- **Provenance:**
  - the node must be present in the decoded grid, otherwise the retrieval is refused (§5.6);
  - no land-sea selection is made: the node is used whether it is land or sea;
  - no land-sea mask is retrieved (§1);
  - nodes are not aligned to Open-Meteo's historical cells.
- **Recorded per point** in the manifest:
  - the requested coordinates;
  - the node coordinates;
  - the **distance** (haversine, km, Earth radius 6371.0 km);
  - `tie_break_applied` (true/false);
  - the note "nearest 0.25° CDS node; not land-cell selected; may differ from the Open-Meteo cell used for the
    historical ERA5 values".

### 5.3 30 km eligibility
- A point is eligible only if its node distance is **≤ 30 km** (VM §6 rule 4). An ineligible point keeps its hourly
  values in the hourly table, but **every one of its IST-day values is null with reason G** ("ERA5 cell > 30 km").
- For the current 36 points the node distances are 2.5–16.5 km (computed), so no point is currently ineligible.
  The rule and its test are kept unchanged.

### 5.4 IST-day aggregation and the UTC → IST boundary

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| IST calendar day D | 00:00–24:00 IST = 18:30Z (D−1) … 18:30Z (D) |
| ERA5 hours | stamped at full UTC hours (validity time); each value is the sum over the preceding hour (t−1 h, t] |
| Stamps used for day D | the **24 stamps 19:00Z (D−1), 20:00Z, …, 18:00Z (D)** |
| Interval actually covered | 18:00Z (D−1) … 18:00Z (D) = **23:30 IST (D−1) … 23:30 IST (D)** |
| Month boundary | for d₁, the stamps 19:00Z–23:00Z (d₁−1) come from the **boundary** request, and 00:00Z–18:00Z (d₁) from the **month** request (§4.1). The two requests are one retrieval and must both pass every check |
| Known offset (limitation) | the ERA5 day starts and ends **30 minutes earlier** than the IST calendar day. Hourly data cannot be split at :30. Each result using D2 states the offset. It is the same convention as the M4.4-B ERA5 rain day |
| Not known | how Open-Meteo builds CORE's own daily rain for Asia/Kolkata (+5:30); D2 does not assume it matches |
| Units | hourly value mm = GRIB value (m) × 1000, in float64 |
| Daily value | the sum of the 24 hourly mm values, computed in float64 and stored as float32 |
| Values as delivered | no clipping, rounding (other than float32 storage), smoothing or filling. Any negative hourly value is kept as delivered and counted in the gap report (§15, item 7) |

### 5.5 Completeness requirement
- A day has a value **only if all 24 stamps exist and all 24 values at the point's node are non-missing**.
- A GRIB bitmap-missing value is a missing hour.
- A day with fewer than 24 non-missing values is null, and records `hours_present` (0–24).

### 5.6 Missing-data handling and structural validation

| Case | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Hour missing at a node in a structurally valid retrieval | day null, reason **E** ("ERA5 hours missing: n of 24"); never zero, never interpolated, never filled from another source or model |
| Node > 30 km | day null, reason **G** |
| Structurally invalid retrieval | **the retrieval is refused** (reason **D**); nothing published (§8) |

A retrieval is structurally valid only if **all** of the following hold for both of its files:
- each is a GRIB file that ecCodes decodes completely;
- every message is `shortName = tp` in metres;
- the set of validity stamps equals exactly the requested stamps (5, and n × 24), each present once;
- every message's grid is the regular 0.25° grid covering the requested area and contains all 36 nodes;
- **every message has `expver = 0001`** (§6).

Otherwise the retrieval is refused, with the failing check named.

- E-reasons are published: they describe a valid source gap, not a failed retrieval. Every E-day is listed in the
  gap report, and any E-day is reported to the owner in the run summary.
- Downstream, a null day is never a negative event (M4.4-D-SPEC-1.0 §16).
- **Owner clarification (30 Sep 2026).** A monthly release is published when the monthly retrieval is structurally
  valid. Days with incomplete hourly coverage are marked `E` with the specific missing-data reason (hours present,
  n of 24). These E-days are:
  - excluded from M4.4-D verification metrics;
  - never converted to zero;
  - never treated as forecast misses.

  The missing-day information stays in the manifest (gap report, listed with its SHA-256) and in the archive-index
  record (§10.4). The verification sample can therefore always tell source missingness from forecast failure.

## 6. Final-data rule (decision R1, revised)

- **Eligibility date (a start date, not proof of finality).** No request for month M is submitted earlier than
  **70 days after M's last day**. A request made earlier is refused before any CDS call.
- **Acceptance test (the actual finality check):**
  - **every requested hourly value** in both requests of the retrieval must carry **`expver = 0001`**. This includes
    the boundary hours of M−1 and the unused last-evening hours of M;
  - **any `expver = 0005` (ERA5T), or any missing or other `expver`, refuses that retrieval.** Nothing from it is
    published;
  - **ERA5T and final ERA5 are never mixed within a release.** A release contains only `expver = 0001` values.
- **Meaning of `expver = 0001`.** It means **final ERA5 as served by the CDS at retrieval time**. It does **not**
  prevent ECMWF from correcting final ERA5 later; ECMWF has done so before. Such a correction is handled only through
  §10.5, as a new BWI version.
- **Stable releases.** A published ERA5 release is never mutated or replaced. The objective is one stable,
  reproducible reference dataset per month.
- **A refusal because of ERA5T** consumes that retrieval attempt (§7). It is recorded, and the owner is told.
  Retrying needs a new committed request (§9).

**Earliest eligibility dates.** These are a consequence of R1, not a readiness projection and not a finality claim:

| Month | IST days | Earliest eligibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-09 | 30 | 9 Dec 2026 |
| 2026-10 | 31 | 9 Jan 2027 |
| 2026-11 | 30 | 8 Feb 2027 |
| 2026-12 | 31 | 11 Mar 2027 |
| 2027-01 | 31 | 11 Apr 2027 |

At the earliest M4.4-D review point (27 Dec 2026), only September 2026 can have an ERA5 reference release.

## 7. Retrieval limit and job ledger (replaces the Open-Meteo call budget)

The Open-Meteo counted-call budget of revision 2 (≤ 200 counted calls per calendar month) **is removed**. It has no
meaning on the CDS.

### 7.1 Limit
- **At most two retrievals per reference month** in normal D2 operation. A retrieval is the pair of CDS requests in
  §4.1, so a month has **at most four CDS jobs**.
- **In the R2 validation month** the controlled repeat (§11) **is** the second retrieval. That month has no further
  retry slot.
- **In any other month** the second retrieval is available only after the first has definitively failed (§8). It is
  started only by a new committed request (§9), never automatically inside a run.
- **No third retrieval ever starts automatically.** The workflow refuses to submit a third retrieval for a month. Any
  later retrieval of a published month is a §10.5 correction, which requires owner approval.
- **A retrieval counts once its first submission intent is recorded** (§7.3), whatever happens afterwards.

### 7.2 Why jobs, not bytes or items, are the limit
CDS limits on items, cost and concurrency are not currently published (§15). D2 therefore limits the only quantity it
controls: the number of jobs it submits. Items, bytes and durations are **recorded**, not budgeted.

### 7.3 Job ledger (append-only, archive-index)
`reference/era5/jobs/YYYY-MM.jsonl` holds one line per event. Lines are never edited or deleted.

| Event | Fields |
| --- | --- |
| `intent` (written **before** the submit call) | month, retrieval number (1 or 2), role (boundary/month), request SHA-256, item count, run id, UTC time |
| `submitted` | the above, plus the `request_id` returned by CDS |
| `status` | `request_id`, CDS status (queued / running / successful / failed), UTC time |
| `downloaded` | `request_id`, bytes, file SHA-256, download duration |
| `closed` | `request_id`, outcome (published / refused: reason / pending), total and queue duration |

The requested items, downloaded bytes and request duration for every job are therefore always recorded, including
for refused retrievals.

## 8. Request-ID workflow, resume and bounded retries

### 8.1 Submit / record / poll / resume

1. **Before submitting anything**, read the month's job ledger:
   - If a job for (month, retrieval, role) has a recorded `request_id` and is not closed: **do not submit**. Resume
     it by polling that `request_id`.
   - If a job has an `intent` but no `request_id`, the submission outcome is unknown: **do not submit again** for
     that retrieval. The retrieval is marked "submission uncertain" and closed as refused, and the owner is told. This
     is the only way to guarantee that a lost response never creates a duplicate CDS job.
2. **Submit** the boundary request, then the month request, each exactly once. Write the `intent` line before each
   call and the `submitted` line immediately after the `request_id` is returned.
3. **Poll** by `request_id` only (§8.2). A queued or running job is never resubmitted.
4. **Download only after the CDS status is successful**, by `request_id`.
5. **Validate** (§5.6, §6); then build, check and publish (§10). Otherwise refuse (§8.3).

### 8.2 Workflow timeout
- The workflow sets a poll deadline below the GitHub Actions job limit.
- If a job is still queued or running at the deadline, the run ends with outcome **pending**. The ledger keeps the
  `request_id`, and no refusal or retry is recorded.
- A later run started by a new commit of the request file (§9) finds the open `request_id` in the ledger and **resumes
  polling it**. A timeout therefore never creates a duplicate CDS job and never consumes a second retrieval.

### 8.3 Bounded retry policy
Automatic retries in the CDS client library are **disabled or capped to this policy**. The library defaults (e.g.
`cdsapi`'s `retry_max = 500`) are not inherited. The waits and caps below are D2 design parameters, not CDS limits.

| Situation | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| **Transport failure** (connection error, read timeout) **during submit** | no resubmission. The retrieval is "submission uncertain" (§8.1 step 1) and refused; the owner is told |
| **Transport failure during poll or download** | retry the same `request_id` up to **3 times**, waiting 30 s, 60 s, then 120 s. If all fail, the run ends **pending**; resume later (§8.2) |
| **HTTP 4xx on submit** (400 invalid request, 401 authentication, 403 licence not accepted or "cost limits exceeded") | **stop; no retry.** The retrieval is refused with the HTTP status and message (secrets removed) |
| **HTTP 429 on poll or download** | wait **120 s**, then continue within the same 3-retry cap |
| **Other HTTP 4xx on poll or download** (e.g. 404 result expired) | stop. The retrieval is refused; there is no resubmission |
| **HTTP 5xx on submit** | treated as "submission uncertain" (the job may exist); no resubmission. Refused; the owner is told |
| **HTTP 5xx on poll or download** | retry the same `request_id` within the 3-retry cap, then **pending** |
| **Queued / running** | poll every **60 s**, doubling to at most **600 s** between polls, until the poll deadline (§8.2) |
| **CDS job failed** | the retrieval is refused, with the CDS error text; there is no resubmission |
| **Completed** | download once, by `request_id` |
| **Malformed or incomplete download** (size ≠ announced length, truncated GRIB, ecCodes decode error) | re-download the **same result** up to **2 more times**. If it still fails, the retrieval is refused. The job is never resubmitted for this reason |
| **Structural or `expver` validation failure** | the retrieval is refused (§5.6, §6); there is no retry |

- **A refused retrieval publishes nothing.** A refusal record (§10.4) stores:
  - the reason and the failing check;
  - the `request_id`s;
  - items, bytes and durations;
  - the error texts, without secrets;
  - the code commit.
- A draft left by an interrupted publication is deleted before the refusal is recorded. A published release is never
  deleted.

## 9. Trigger (no schedule)

- A month is retrieved **only** through an explicit committed request: a file `reference/era5_request.json` on the
  review branch `m4-history`, containing `{"months": ["YYYY-MM"], "purpose": "…", "repeat_check": false}`.
- Committing a change to that file starts one GitHub Actions run of the D2 workflow for exactly the listed months.
- For each month, the run first resumes any open `request_id` in the ledger (§8.1). It submits a new retrieval only if
  none is open and the retrieval limit (§7.1) allows it.
- **There is no cron schedule.** No run starts without a new commit to the request file.
- The workflow runs on `m4-history` and never on `main`. It never touches the daily archive or its workflow.
- The CDS personal access token is a GitHub Actions secret. The CDS account, the manual acceptance of the dataset
  terms and the secret are **owner tasks before implementation**. None of them exists yet.

## 10. Versions, provenance and releases

### 10.1 Release naming
- One immutable GitHub release per month: **`ref-era5-YYYY-MM`**.
- A correction is `ref-era5-YYYY-MM-v2` (then `-v3` …), per §10.5.

### 10.2 Release files

| File | Content |
| --- | --- |
| `era5_cds_boundary_YYYY-MM.grib` | the downloaded boundary file, unmodified |
| `era5_cds_month_YYYY-MM.grib` | the downloaded month file, unmodified |
| `era5_cds_requests_YYYY-MM.json` | the two canonical request dicts, their SHA-256 values and `request_id`s |
| `era5_hourly_YYYY-MM.parquet` | point_id, utc_time, precipitation_mm (nullable), node_lat, node_lon, expver, source_request (boundary/month) |
| `era5_ist_day_YYYY-MM.parquet` | point_id, ist_date, precip_ist_day_mm (nullable), hours_present, reason (null / E / G), node_distance_km, eligible |
| `gap_report_ref-era5-YYYY-MM.json` | expected versus present hours per point, every E- and G-day, and the count of any negative hourly values |
| `manifest_ref-era5-YYYY-MM.json` | §10.3 |

### 10.3 Manifest contents
- **Dataset:** `dataset: "era5_reference_supplement"`, `role: "secondary rain reference (interim)"`, `month`,
  `schema_version`.
- **Source:**
  - `service: "Copernicus Climate Data Store"`;
  - `cds_dataset: "reanalysis-era5-single-levels"`, `product_type: "reanalysis"`,
    `variable: "total_precipitation"`, `data_format: "grib"`;
  - the two canonical request dicts, their SHA-256 values and `request_id`s;
  - the GRIB base-time/step encoding observed.
- **Version:**
  - `expver: "0001"` (all values; the check result);
  - "final ERA5 as served by the CDS at retrieval time; retrieved N days after month end (≥ 70); does not exclude
    later ECMWF corrections".
- **Retrieval:** per job, the submission / start / completion / download UTC times, the queue and total duration, the
  items requested, the bytes downloaded, the file SHA-256, the HTTP statuses and the retrieval number (1 or 2).
- **Coverage:** the IST dates covered (d₁ … dₙ), the UTC stamps used (19:00Z d₀ … 18:00Z dₙ), and the stamps
  requested but unused.
- **Code:**
  - the code commit (git SHA);
  - the workflow run URL;
  - the request-file commit;
  - the CDS client and ecCodes versions.
- **Points:**
  - the `archive/points.json` SHA-256 and the area box;
  - per point, the requested and node coordinates, distance, `tie_break_applied`, eligible flag, and the note from
    §5.2.
- **Aggregation:** the rule text (§5.4), including the 30-minute offset statement and the m → mm conversion.
- **Licence and attribution:** §10.6.
- **Files:** for every file, its name, bytes, rows (tables) and **SHA-256**.
- **Repeat check:** `repeat_check`, and its result reference if one was performed (§11).

### 10.4 Checksums and archive-index record
- Before publication, every file is hashed and checked against the manifest. After publication, every asset is
  downloaded again and checked, and the attempt to add, overwrite or delete an asset must be rejected (immutability
  verified).
- Append-only archive-index records:
  - `reference/era5/index/YYYY-MM.json`: manifest SHA-256, release URL, checksum and immutability check results,
    the list of E- and G-days (point, IST date, reason, hours present), the `request_id`s, run id;
  - `reference/era5/refused/YYYY-MM_<run>.json` for every refusal;
  - `reference/era5/jobs/YYYY-MM.jsonl`: the job ledger (§7.3);
  - `reference/era5/validation/YYYY-MM_repeat.json`: the R2 evidence (§11).
- Existing records are never overwritten.

### 10.5 Correction procedure
- A published month is corrected **only** for a documented defect: an extraction bug, or a documented upstream
  ERA5 correction. The owner must approve it, and a committed request marks it as a correction.
- The correction is a new release `ref-era5-YYYY-MM-v2`. Its manifest carries `supersedes`, the reason, and a
  value-level comparison with the previous version.
- The old release stays immutable and remains the reference for any result that used it. Every downstream result
  names the exact release it used.

### 10.6 Licence and attribution
- **Manifest:**
  - `licence: "Licence to use Copernicus Products (CC-BY per CDS dataset page)"`;
  - the date on which the owner accepted the dataset terms (as stated by the owner);
  - the attribution texts below.
- **Attribution:**
  - for the unmodified GRIB files: "Generated using Copernicus Climate Change Service information [Year]";
  - for the derived tables (unit conversion and aggregation): "Contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service
    information [Year]";
  - plus the statement "Neither the European Commission nor ECMWF is responsible for any use that may be made of the
    Copernicus information or data it contains".
  - `[Year]` is filled with the retrieval year (§15, item 8).
- **Website / data-source documentation:** at implementation the same attribution is added to the V2 data-source
  documentation and to any website page that shows D2-derived results. That change is part of the implementation
  approval, not of this plan revision.

### 10.7 Two different kinds of "change", never confused
- **Immutable BWI evidence release:** `ref-era5-YYYY-MM` records exactly what the CDS served at retrieval time. It is
  never altered, and remains valid evidence of what BWI used.
- **A future ECMWF correction to ERA5:** an upstream change after publication. It does **not** alter or invalidate
  the BWI release. If the owner decides to adopt it, it enters only as a new version (§10.5). Results already produced
  keep naming the version they used.

## 11. Reproducibility check (decision R2)

- **Routine retrievals are single-pass**, validated by the structural, `expver`, checksum, completeness and
  manifest checks. There are no routine duplicate retrievals.
- **One controlled repeat, during D2 validation only.**
  - For one selected month (the first month retrieved), a first complete retrieval is followed by a second identical
    retrieval: the same two canonical request dicts, as new CDS jobs.
  - The repeat is that month's second retrieval (§7.1).
  - It runs after the first retrieval has completed and passed validation.
- **GRIB files are not compared byte for byte.** Server metadata and encoding may differ.
- **Value-level equality criterion (explicit):**
  - **Identical structure:** the same set of validity stamps, the same grid and node coordinates, `expver = 0001`
    everywhere in both, the same missing-value positions, and the same E/G reasons and `hours_present` per point-day.
  - **Hourly values:** for every point and stamp, `|v₁ − v₂| ≤ q₁/2 + q₂/2`, where `v` is the decoded value in metres
    and `q` is the packing step of that GRIB message (the decoding precision implied by its own encoding parameters:
    reference value, binary and decimal scale factors, bits per value). Values that decode identically trivially
    meet this.
  - **IST-day totals and monthly totals per point:** the difference must not exceed the sum of the per-hour bounds of
    the hours summed.
  - The criterion is derived from the files' own encoding. It is a reproducibility test, not a new meteorological
    threshold.
- **Recorded:**
  - the maximum absolute hourly, daily and monthly differences (mm);
  - whether the values were byte-identical after decoding;
  - the packing steps;
  - the SHA-256 of both canonical hourly and daily tables.
  The evidence goes to `reference/era5/validation/YYYY-MM_repeat.json` and is referenced in that month's manifest.
- **If any criterion fails** (a material difference): the month is **not published** until the difference is
  investigated and reported to the owner. No third retrieval is started (§7.1).
- **Which retrieval is published:** the first retrieval's files, when the criterion passes. The repeat's `request_id`s
  and file SHA-256 values are recorded as evidence only.

   and R2 (one controlled repeat during validation only). **Done: §6 and §11.**
2. The owner has selected the acquisition route: **Option C, direct Copernicus ERA5** (30 Sep 2026). **Done: §3 and
   §14.** The Open-Meteo non-commercial confirmation is no longer a D2 prerequisite. The status of the historical M4.2
   archive remains a separate open item.
3. The owner accepts the two-request retrieval structure (§4), the fixed node rule and tie-break (§5.2), the
   retrieval limit and ledger (§7), and the request-ID workflow and retry policy (§8).
4. The owner accepts the release naming, file set, manifest fields, archive-index records, correction procedure,
   licence/attribution handling and the evidence-versus-upstream distinction (§10).
5. The owner accepts the IST-day aggregation, including the stated 30-minute offset (§5.4), and the E/G missing-data
   treatment (§5.6).
6. **Owner tasks before implementation (not done):** create the CDS account, accept the dataset terms on the dataset
   page, and store the personal access token as a GitHub Actions secret.

### B. For a later D2 implementation (after approval)
1. **Exact CDS dataset and request:**
   - the two request dicts are built exactly as in §4, unit-tested against fixed expected canonical JSON for a
     30-day month, a 31-day month, February, and January (the year boundary);
   - their SHA-256 values and `request_id`s are in the manifest;
   - no other dataset, product type, variable or format is requestable.
2. **Final data only:**
   - a known-answer test with one `expver = 0005` message anywhere (boundary or month file, used or unused stamp)
     refuses the retrieval and publishes nothing;
   - a missing `expver` also refuses;
   - a request before the 70-day eligibility date is refused before any CDS call.
3. **Complete hourly coverage:** a file missing or duplicating any requested stamp is refused (D). A bitmap-missing
   hour at a node gives E for that day, never zero.
4. **Correct IST aggregation:**
   - known-answer tests for the 19:00Z (D−1) … 18:00Z (D) window;
   - d₁ stitched from the boundary and month files;
   - the m → mm conversion;
   - float64 summation with float32 storage;
   - 23 hours → null with E.
5. **36-point extraction:** 36 point ids in `points.json` order; the manifest records the file SHA-256 and the area
   box; the box is derived by the §4.2 rule.
6. **30 km rule:** a known-answer test with a node over 30 km gives G for every day; exactly 30.0 km is eligible.
7. **Fixed tie-break:**
   - IN-11-243 maps to 27.25° N, 88.50° E with `tie_break_applied = true`;
   - synthetic midpoints in latitude and longitude go north and east;
   - decimal, not binary-float, evaluation is tested.
8. **Request-ID persistence and resume:**
   - an interrupted run (simulated timeout while queued) followed by a new run resumes the recorded `request_id` and
     submits nothing new;
   - an `intent` without a `request_id` is never resubmitted;
   - the ledger is append-only.
9. **Bounded retries:** fault-injection tests for every row of §8.3 show the specified number of attempts, the waits
   (mocked clock), no resubmission after transport failure or 5xx on submit, and no retry on 4xx. Library-level
   retries are shown to be disabled or capped.
10. **Two-retrieval maximum:** a test shows a third retrieval for a month is refused before any CDS call, and that the
    R2 month has no retry slot.
11. **R2 value-level comparison:**
    - performed once in validation, with the §11 criterion implemented and tested:
      - identical decode passes;
      - a difference within the packing bound passes;
      - a difference above the bound, or any structural or `expver` difference, blocks publication;
    - the evidence is recorded.
12. **Manifest and checksums:** every file's SHA-256 matches the manifest before and after publication; a tampered
    file is refused.
13. **Immutable publication:** after publication, attempts to add, overwrite and delete an asset are rejected, and
    GitHub's immutable flag is true.
14. **Archive-index provenance:** the index, job-ledger, refusal and validation records exist and are append-only,
    with no record overwritten. Every job's items, bytes and durations are recorded.
15. **No partial release:** fault-injection tests (submit failure, poll failure, failed job, malformed download,
    `expver` refusal, validation failure, interrupted publication) leave no release and no draft, and do leave a
    refusal or pending record.
16. **Licence and attribution:** the manifest carries §10.6. The data-source documentation carries the attribution.
17. **Reproducibility from the record:** from the manifest alone (the request dicts, dataset id, `points.json` hash,
    node rule, aggregation text and code commit), an independent run can rebuild the request, re-derive the tables
    from the released GRIB files byte-identically, and re-request the data.
18. **Regression — nothing else changed:**
    - CORE still at `2840f8d` (`core-v1.0`);
    - `main` / V2 unchanged by D2;
    - M4.4 methodology (VM-1.1, M4.4-D-SPEC-1.0) unchanged;
    - all existing releases (history-*, archive-daily-*, legacy) still immutable with unchanged checksums;
    - the recorded M4.4-A/B/C and census result hashes unchanged;
    - offline suites (archive, history, verification, api-v2) and CI pass.

## 15. Unresolved items (not invented; to be measured or confirmed)

1. **CDS item and cost limits.** The current numeric limits for ERA5 GRIB requests are not published. The last
   published figure is 120,000 items per request (2019). D2's largest request is 744 items.
2. **Queue and concurrency.** Per-user concurrency limits and queue times are not published. The poll deadline
   (§8.2) is set at implementation, and actual queue times are recorded.
3. **Result retention.** How long a completed CDS result remains downloadable is not published. An expired result
   refuses the retrieval (§8.3), without resubmission.
4. **Final-data timing.** Whether 70 days after month end always falls after ECMWF's final-ERA5 overwrite. It is
   detected by `expver`, not assumed.
5. **Delivered GRIB encoding.**
   - The base-time/step layout of `tp` messages, and the exact ecCodes keys for validity time and `expver` in files
     served by the current CDS, are confirmed on the first retrieval.
   - The implementation must refuse, not guess, if they differ from §3.
6. **Duplicate-job detection.** Whether the CDS API returns a submitted job's request parameters (which would allow
   an "intent without `request_id`" to be matched to an existing job) is not verified. Until it is, such a retrieval
   is closed as refused (§8.1).
7. **Negative values.** Whether ERA5 `tp` served by the CDS contains any negative hourly values at these nodes.
   Values are kept as delivered and counted (§5.4). Any treatment beyond that needs an owner decision.
8. **Attribution `[Year]`.** The plan uses the retrieval year. The licence text's intended meaning of `[Year]` is to
   be confirmed when implementation is approved.
9. **Client retry parameters.** The exact parameter names for disabling or capping library-level retries in the
   chosen client (`ecmwf-datastores-client` or `cdsapi` ≥ 0.7.7) are confirmed at implementation.
10. **Node differences from the historical ERA5 values.** Which nodes differ from the Open-Meteo cells used for the
    M4.4-B ERA5 values is measurable only on the first retrieval. The differences are recorded, not corrected.
